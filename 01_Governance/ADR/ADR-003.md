# ADR-003 — Repository Authority Relationship Between Architecture, Product Requirements, and Specifications

**Document ID:** ADR-003
**Version:** 1.0.0
**Status:** Approved
**Owner:** Chief Architect / Product Owner
**Depends On:** CONST-001, ADR-002
**Related Artifacts:** GOV-001, Project Charter, PRD, Architecture
**Date:** 2026-08-07

## 1. Status

Approved.

## 2. Context

ADR-002 intentionally deferred the authority relationship between Architecture, Product Requirements, and Specifications. GOV-001 requires that relationship to be explicit so conflicts can be resolved deterministically. This ADR resolves only that authority relationship; it neither introduces new concept ownership nor modifies Governance Principles or the Concept Registry.

## 3. Decision

| Authority Level | Artifact Category |
|---:|---|
| 3 | Architecture |
| 4 | Product Requirements |
| 5 | Specifications |

The authority chain is **Architecture → Product Requirements → Specifications**. Each lower level conforms to governing decisions of the level above within its scope.

### 3.1 Authority Boundaries

Architecture defines system structure, architectural constraints, component relationships, and cross-cutting architectural patterns. It does not define product capability requirements.

Product Requirements define Product Goals, Product Capability Scope, Functional Requirements, and product-specific Non-Functional Requirements. Product-specific Non-Functional Requirements shall reference the canonical Quality Attribute Catalog owned by GOV-001 and shall not redefine quality dimensions. The PRD conforms to architectural constraints and does not redefine architecture.

Specifications define implementation-independent realization of approved Product Requirements. They conform to Architecture and Product Requirements and do not redefine either.

## 4. Rationale

The decision separates system design, product intent, and detailed specification. Architecture establishes permissible structures; the PRD defines capabilities within those constraints; Specifications describe those capabilities for implementation without redefining architecture or product intent. This preserves traceability and prevents circular authority.

## 5. Consequences

- The Authority Model is complete for these artifact categories.
- Conflict resolution is deterministic.
- Architecture and product ownership remain distinct.
- Changes to these authority relationships require a new ADR.

## 6. Alternatives Considered

### Alternative A — Product Requirements above Architecture

Rejected. Product requirements could override architectural constraints and increase architecture drift.

### Alternative B — Architecture and Product Requirements as peers

Rejected. Peer authority would create conflict-resolution ambiguity.

### Alternative C — Specifications above Product Requirements

Rejected. A realization artifact cannot outrank the requirements it realizes.
